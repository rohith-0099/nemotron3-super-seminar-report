\section{Background}

Almost every large language model (LLM) in use today is built on the
Transformer architecture introduced by Vaswani et al.\ in
2017~\cite{vaswani2017attention}. Its central operation, self-attention, lets
every token look directly at every other token. That flexibility has a price:
the computation of an attention layer grows quadratically with the input
length, and during generation the model must keep the keys and values of all
previous tokens in a \emph{key--value (KV) cache} that grows with every token.
For a short chat this is negligible; for an AI agent that reads an entire code
repository or a several-hundred-page document it becomes the dominant cost.

Two lines of research attack this cost. \emph{State-space models} (SSMs) such
as Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba} and Mamba-2~\cite{dao2024mamba2} summarise
everything seen so far in a state of fixed size, so time grows linearly with
the sequence length and generation memory does not grow at all.
\emph{Mixture-of-Experts} (MoE) layers~\cite{shazeer2017moe,lepikhin2020gshard}
split the feed-forward part of a model into many small experts and activate
only a few per token, so a model can hold many parameters while spending the
computation of a much smaller one.

\NS, released by NVIDIA in April 2026~\cite{nvidia2026super}, combines both
ideas in one open model. It has 120.6~billion parameters, of which only
12.7~billion are active per token. Most of its 88 layers are Mamba-2 and MoE
layers; only eight are attention layers. It accepts contexts of up to one
million tokens and reaches accuracy comparable to
GPT-OSS-120B~\cite{openai2025gptoss} and Qwen3.5-122B with up to 2.2$\times$
and 7.5$\times$ higher inference throughput respectively.

\section{Motivation}

\NS\ is the clearest example so far of a production-scale LLM that is
\emph{not} a pure Transformer. Earlier hybrids were small or research
prototypes; this model shows that the approach scales beyond one hundred
billion parameters and to million-token contexts while staying competitive with
the strongest open Transformers. Studying it answers a broader question: is
the attention-only design still the best foundation for large models? The
model is also fully open---weights, much of the data and the training recipe
are published---so its engineering can be studied in detail.

\section{Objectives of the Seminar}

\begin{itemize}
  \item To understand why Transformers become slow and memory-heavy on long
        inputs, and how state-space models address this.
  \item To study how \NS\ interleaves Mamba-2 and attention layers, and how
        LatentMoE keeps a large model cheap to run.
  \item To review how the model is trained, quantized and evaluated, and what
        the design suggests about the future of efficient large-scale AI.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope}

The report is a study of published work. Its primary source is the \NS\
technical report~\cite{nvidia2026super}; the supporting papers on Mamba,
Mamba-2, LatentMoE, multi-token prediction and NVFP4 training are used to
explain the building blocks in more depth. No new experiments were run. Where
the report contains estimates worked out by the author from published
configuration values, this is stated explicitly.
