\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter summarises the results published in the technical
report~\cite{nvidia2026super}, comparing \NS\ with two open MoE Transformers
of similar size: GPT-OSS-120B~\cite{openai2025gptoss} and Qwen3.5-122B-A10B.

\section{Base Model}

Before post-training, the base model is the most accurate of three public base
models on almost every task (Table~\ref{tab:base}), with the largest margins on
hard reasoning and long-context retrieval. It is the only one evaluated beyond
128K tokens, scoring 71.0 on RULER at one million tokens.

\NS\ matches or beats GPT-OSS-120B on most benchmarks, with the largest leads
on agentic tasks (60.5 against 41.9 on SWE-Bench), reflecting its agentic
post-training. Qwen3.5-122B is more accurate on most rows, including GPQA,
SWE-Bench and TauBench; the report itself says \NS\ lags it slightly on
reasoning. The fair summary is that the hybrid sits in the same accuracy class
as the best open Transformers of its size, and its advantage is speed.

\section{Long Context}

On RULER~\cite{hsieh2024ruler} (Figure~\ref{fig:ruler}) \NS\ loses only about
five points from 256k to one million tokens, ending at 91.6, while
GPT-OSS-120B falls to 22.3. Qwen3.5-122B tracks \NS\ closely, so long-context
\emph{accuracy} is not exclusive to hybrids; what the hybrid changes is the
\emph{cost} of serving such contexts. Post-training raised the 1M score from
71.0 to 91.6.

\section{Inference Throughput}

With 8k input and 64k output tokens on B200 GPUs, \NS\ in NVFP4 delivers up to
2.2$\times$ the throughput of GPT-OSS-120B (MXFP4) and 7.5$\times$ that of
Qwen3.5-122B (BF16). The 2.2$\times$ figure depends on NVFP4 quantization: in
BF16, \NS\ runs at about 0.6$\times$ GPT-OSS-120B. At equal BF16 precision it
is still about twice as fast as Qwen3.5-122B despite activating more
parameters, because long generations are exactly where a constant-size Mamba
state beats a growing KV cache (Table~\ref{tab:throughput}).

\section{Summary of Results}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The base model outperforms comparable public base models on nearly all
        benchmarks.
  \item The post-trained model matches or beats GPT-OSS-120B on most tasks and
        trails Qwen3.5-122B on several reasoning and agentic benchmarks.
  \item It scores 91.6 on RULER at one million tokens, against 22.3 for
        GPT-OSS-120B.
  \item It runs up to 2.2$\times$ faster than GPT-OSS-120B and up to
        7.5$\times$ faster than Qwen3.5-122B.
  \item NVFP4 quantization keeps a median of 99.8\% of BF16 accuracy, and MTP
        accepts 3.45 tokens per step on average.
\end{itemize}

Taken together, the results support the central claim of the hybrid design.
Replacing most attention layers with Mamba-2 does not cost accuracy compared
with Transformer models of the same class, keeps long-context retrieval strong
up to one million tokens, and delivers its largest benefit in exactly the
long-generation and agentic settings that are becoming the main use of large
language models. The comparison with Qwen3.5-122B also shows that accuracy
still depends heavily on data and post-training, not on architecture alone.
